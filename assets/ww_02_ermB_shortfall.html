The ermB Shortfall in Effluent

In three of nine effluent samples our ermB total is far below the published one, while the other four genes in the same samples agree. This page shows where the reads went and what the follow-up tests do and do not explain. The ermB work was exploratory and done after the scoring; it does not change the registered result.

What the follow-up tests show

With competition (full database).
E021: erm(B)_2 receives 0 reads; the control E026 receives 698. Reads are assigned to erm(B)_18, erm(B)_20, erm(G)_1 or erm(F)_3 instead.
Without competition (erm(B)_2 alone, raw reads).
E021: 899 reads, 738 of 738 positions covered. E026: 906 reads, 738 of 738. The two samples look alike.

The single-template count is an upper bound: any sufficiently similar read, including from related erm genes, can map to it. It shows that sequence similar to erm(B)_2 is present, not how much of it there is.

The same samples, all five genes

Ratio of our gene total to the published gene total, evaluation samples. Dashes mean the paper reports no count for that gene in that sample. Green is within 0.90–1.10; red is outside.

Gene-level agreement is strong everywhere except ermB in two samples. At subtype level, counts swap between near-identical alleles, which is the same effect seen here at larger scale.

The paper’s text describes a whole-gene coverage requirement, yet its published counts contain thin blocks of the kind shown above, so low-support counts should not be read as abundance. Source: R1_ermB_diagnostic_note.md and ermB_diagnostic_effluent.tsv in the GitHub repository. Research use only.
